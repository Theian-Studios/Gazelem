# Asked and Answered
* subtitle: Doctrine and Covenants revelations that came as responses to specific questions — compiled and verified from the text
* volume: doctrine-and-covenants
* order: 1
* columns: Section | The question or prompt | What came back
* widths: 0.6 | 1.7 | 1.8

A remarkable share of the Doctrine and Covenants exists because someone asked something. Some questions are preserved in the text itself; where the prompt is known only from the historical record, the chart says so.

- **D&C 3, 10** | The lost 116 pages — Martin Harris begged to show the manuscript and lost it: “thou deliveredst up that which was sacred into the hands of a wicked man” (3:12). | Rebuke, reassurance, and the workaround: do not retranslate — the small plates of Nephi cover the same period, prepared “for a wise purpose” against exactly this scheme (10:30–45).
- **D&C 6** | Oliver Cowdery’s unspoken doubt about the work. | “Cast your mind upon the night that you cried unto me in your heart… Did I not speak peace to your mind concerning the matter? What greater witness can you have than from God?” (6:22–23) — an answer referencing a prayer Oliver had told no one about.
- **D&C 7** | Joseph and Oliver disagreed: did the apostle John die, or does he still live? | The answer came as a translated parchment of John’s own words — “Lord, give unto me power over death, that I may live and bring souls unto thee” — John tarries (7:1–3).
- **D&C 8, 9** | Oliver asked to translate; then asked why he failed. | First, the definition of revelation: “I will tell you in your mind and in your heart” (8:2). Then the diagnosis: “you took no thought save it was to ask me… you must study it out in your mind” (9:7–8).
- **D&C 76** | While revising the Bible, Joseph and Sidney reached John 5:29 on the two resurrections and “meditated upon these things” (76:15–19). | “The Lord touched the eyes of our understandings and they were opened” — the vision of the three degrees of glory.
- **D&C 77** | A string of questions about the book of Revelation. | The only section written as literal Q&A: “Q. What is the sea of glass…? A. It is the earth, in its sanctified, immortal, and eternal state” (77:1).
- **D&C 89** | The School of the Prophets’ tobacco use and Emma’s complaints prompted Joseph to inquire (from the historical record; the text calls itself “a principle with promise, adapted to the capacity of the weak,” 89:3). | The Word of Wisdom — “not by commandment or constraint, but by revelation” (89:2), ending in the promises of health, wisdom, and deliverance (89:18–21).
- **D&C 91** | Should the Apocrypha be included in the Bible translation? | “There are many things contained therein that are true… it is not needful that the Apocrypha should be translated” — but whoso reads with the Spirit “shall obtain benefit” (91:1–5).
- **D&C 113** | Questions on Isaiah 11. | Q&A again: “Who is the Stem of Jesse…? Verily thus saith the Lord: It is Christ” (113:1–2).
- **D&C 121–123** | The prayer from Liberty Jail: “O God, where art thou? And where is the pavilion that covereth thy hiding place?” (121:1). | “My son, peace be unto thy soul; thine adversity and thine afflictions shall be but a small moment” (121:7) — and the great discourse on priesthood, power, and patience.
- **D&C 132** | The text opens with the question it answers: “you have inquired of my hand to know… wherein I, the Lord, justified my servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob” (132:1). | The revelation on the eternity of the marriage covenant.

## Observations

The pattern across the chart: the Lord almost never just answers. He answers and enlarges — a question about the Apocrypha becomes a principle for reading anything with the Spirit; a question about John 5:29 becomes the entire map of eternity; a cry from a jail cell becomes the church’s charter on power and gentleness.

The chart also traces a doctrine of how revelation works: sections 6, 8, and 9 form a small curriculum — God answers prayers no one else heard, speaks to mind and heart together, and expects the asker to study it out first.
