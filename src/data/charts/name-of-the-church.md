# The Name of the Church — An Evolution
* subtitle: Fayette to Salt Lake City, 1829–2019 — revelation, official action, and the nicknames in between
* volume: doctrine-and-covenants
* order: 2
* columns: When | What happened | Reference
* widths: 0.8 | 2.4 | 0.8

Four threads run through this chart and are worth telling apart as you read it:
revelation and scripture, official action, popular usage and nicknames, and the
long work of correction and emphasis. The name was given by revelation in 1838
and had to be recovered twice — once in law, once in habit.

- Jun 1829 // **In translation** | The risen Christ in 3 Nephi: "ye shall call the church in my name" | 3 Nephi 27:7–8
- 6 Apr 1830 // **Church of Christ** | Organized at Fayette as the Church of Christ | D&C 20:1
- ca. 1831 // **"Mormonites"** | Outsiders coin "Mormonite," soon shortened to "Mormon" |
- 3 May 1834 // **Latter Day Saints** | A Kirtland conference adopts "The Church of the Latter Day Saints" |
- 26 Apr 1838 // **Full name revealed** | At Far West: "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints" | D&C 115:4
- 8 Feb 1851 // **Hyphen fixed** | Incorporated in Deseret; "Latter-day" becomes the legal form |
- 1995 // **Christ made central** | A new wordmark enlarges the name of Jesus Christ |
- 2001 // **Style guide** | Media asked to use the full name; "Mormon Church" discouraged |
- 2010 // **"I'm a Mormon"** | A global media campaign embraces the nickname |
- 16 Aug 2018 // **Course correction** | President Nelson: the Lord impressed the importance of the revealed name |
- 2018–19 // **Names renewed** | The Tabernacle Choir renamed; church websites and domains adopt the full name |
