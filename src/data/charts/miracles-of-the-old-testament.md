# The Miracles of the Old Testament
* subtitle: The mighty works of God, from the Exodus to the Exile — and the hand that wrought each
* volume: old-testament
* order: 7
* columns: The Miracle | Through | Reference (KJV)

The Old Testament is punctuated by signs — moments when the ordinary order of things bent to the word of the Lord. This chart gathers them by era, from the plagues of Egypt to the lions’ den of Babylon. The middle column names the human agent through whom the miracle came, though the power was never his own: the sea obeyed the Lord, not Moses; the fire fell for the Lord, not Elijah.

The two great clusters are unmistakable — the founding miracles of Moses, and the paired ministries of Elijah and Elisha, upon whom “a double portion” of the spirit rested (2 Kings 2:9). Between and after them, the signs grow rarer, until in the exile they blaze up again to prove that Israel’s God still reigns among the nations.

Sixty-two miracles across six eras. The tally is representative rather than complete — the ten plagues are counted as one entry, and summary notices (such as the many healed by looking to the brazen serpent) are folded into single lines. “Through” names the human instrument where the text supplies one, and reads “The Lord” where the wonder came with no named agent; in every case the power was God’s alone.
