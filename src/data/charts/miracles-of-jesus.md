# The Miracles of Jesus Christ
* subtitle: Type, initiative, and the faith connection — across the four Gospels
* volume: new-testament
* order: 5
* columns: Miracle | Initiated By | Faith Noted in the Account? | Reference (KJV)
* widths: 1.7 | 1 | 1 | 1.2

Each entry records who initiated the miracle — the sufferer, a proxy pleading for another, or Jesus unprompted — and whether faith is explicitly named in the account. The initiative column reorganizes the corpus: the unprompted miracles cluster around resurrections and feedings, the things no one dares ask for. Synoptic parallels are combined in single entries.

35 miracles in 4 groups. “Not stated” means the account names no faith on the part of those healed — not that none was present. Summary notices of many healed at once (Matt 4:23–24; 8:16; 15:30) are not itemized. Where accounts differ in number (one blind man or two; one demoniac or two), the entry follows the fuller narrative and notes the parallel.

## Healings
Restorations of the body — sought, brought, and unsought.

- The nobleman’s son at Capernaum | Proxy — the father besought Him at Cana | Yes — “the man believed the word” (Jn 4:50) | John 4:46–54
- Peter’s wife’s mother | Proxy — “they besought him for her” | Not stated | Mark 1:30–31 (Matt 8:14; Lk 4:38)
- A leper | The sufferer — “if thou wilt, thou canst” | Implied in the plea | Mark 1:40–45 (Matt 8:2; Lk 5:12)
- The palsied man borne of four | Proxy — friends through the roof | Yes — “Jesus saw their faith” (Mk 2:5) | Mark 2:3–12 (Matt 9:2; Lk 5:18)
- The impotent man at Bethesda | Jesus unprompted — “Wilt thou be made whole?” | No — the man never asked | John 5:2–9
- The man with the withered hand | Jesus unprompted, in the synagogue, under watching eyes | Not stated | Matt 12:10–13 (Mk 3:1; Lk 6:6)
- The centurion’s servant | Proxy — the centurion (through elders, in Luke) | Yes — “so great faith, no, not in Israel” (Matt 8:10) | Matt 8:5–13 (Lk 7:2–10)
- The woman with an issue of blood | The sufferer — secretly, touching His garment | Yes — “thy faith hath made thee whole” (Mk 5:34) | Mark 5:25–34 (Matt 9:20; Lk 8:43)
- Two blind men in Galilee | The sufferers — “Believe ye that I am able?” | Yes — “according to your faith” (Matt 9:29) | Matt 9:27–31
- The deaf man with an impediment | Proxy — “they bring unto him” (Decapolis) | Not stated | Mark 7:31–37
- The blind man of Bethsaida | Proxy — “they bring a blind man unto him”; healed in two stages | Not stated | Mark 8:22–26
- The man born blind | Jesus unprompted — “that the works of God should be made manifest” | Faith follows the healing (Jn 9:35–38) | John 9:1–7
- The woman bowed eighteen years | Jesus unprompted, in the synagogue | Not stated | Luke 13:10–13
- The man with the dropsy | Jesus unprompted, at the Pharisee’s table | Not stated | Luke 14:1–4
- The ten lepers | The sufferers — “they lifted up their voices” | Yes — to the Samaritan: “thy faith hath made thee whole” (Lk 17:19) | Luke 17:12–19
- Blind Bartimaeus (and his fellow) | The sufferer(s) crying by the wayside | Yes — “thy faith hath made thee whole” (Mk 10:52) | Mark 10:46–52 (Matt 20:30; Lk 18:35)
- Malchus’ ear | Jesus unprompted, at His own arrest | No — healed an enemy | Luke 22:50–51

## Deliverances from Evil Spirits
The devils know Him first — and obey.

- The demoniac in the Capernaum synagogue | The demon cried out — “I know thee who thou art” | Not stated | Mark 1:23–26 (Lk 4:33)
- The blind and dumb demoniac | Proxy — “then was brought unto him”; provoked the Beelzebub charge | Not stated | Matt 12:22
- The dumb demoniac | Proxy — “they brought to him” | Not stated | Matt 9:32–33
- The Gadarene demoniac (Legion) | The possessed man ran to Him; the demons besought | Not stated | Mark 5:1–20 (Matt 8:28; Lk 8:26)
- The Syrophenician woman’s daughter | Proxy — the Gentile mother would not be refused | Yes — “O woman, great is thy faith” (Matt 15:28) | Matt 15:21–28 (Mk 7:24–30)
- The lunatic boy, after the Transfiguration | Proxy — the father: “help thou mine unbelief” | Yes — “if thou canst believe, all things are possible” (Mk 9:23) | Mark 9:17–27 (Matt 17:14; Lk 9:38)

## Raisings of the Dead
The three raisings — and who asked for them.

- Jairus’ daughter | Proxy — the father fell at His feet | Yes — “Be not afraid, only believe” (Mk 5:36) | Mark 5:22–43 (Matt 9:18; Lk 8:41)
- The widow of Nain’s son | Jesus unprompted — “he had compassion on her”; no one asked | No — pure compassion | Luke 7:11–15
- Lazarus of Bethany | Proxy — the sisters sent word; He tarried two days | Yes — “Believest thou this?” (Jn 11:26, 40) | John 11:1–45

## Miracles over Nature and Provision
Wind, sea, bread, and the tree — creation answering its Maker.

- Water made wine at Cana | Prompted by His mother — “they have no wine” | Not stated — “his disciples believed on him” follows (Jn 2:11) | John 2:1–11
- The first draught of fishes | Jesus directed — “Launch out into the deep” | Peter’s obedience against experience (Lk 5:5) | Luke 5:4–11
- Stilling the storm | The disciples woke Him in fear | Rebuked for its absence — “Where is your faith?” (Lk 8:25) | Mark 4:35–41 (Matt 8:23; Lk 8:22)
- Feeding the five thousand | Jesus unprompted — the only miracle in all four Gospels | Not stated | Matt 14:13–21 (Mk 6:32; Lk 9:10; Jn 6:1)
- Walking on the sea | Jesus unprompted; Peter asked to come | Peter’s faith and its failure — “O thou of little faith” (Matt 14:31) | Matt 14:22–33 (Mk 6:45; Jn 6:15)
- Feeding the four thousand | Jesus unprompted — “I have compassion on the multitude” | Not stated | Matt 15:32–39 (Mk 8:1–9)
- The coin in the fish’s mouth | Prompted by the tribute collectors’ question to Peter | Not stated | Matt 17:24–27
- The withered fig tree | Jesus unprompted — an enacted prophecy | Taught from it — “Have faith in God” (Mk 11:22–24) | Mark 11:12–14, 20–24 (Matt 21:18)
- The second draught of fishes | The risen Lord directed — “Cast the net on the right side” | Recognition follows — “It is the Lord” (Jn 21:7) | John 21:1–11
