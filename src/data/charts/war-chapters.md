# The War Chapters at a Glance
* subtitle: A campaign-by-campaign guide to Alma 43–62 (c. 74–60 BC), compiled and verified from the text
* volume: book-of-mormon
* order: 3
* columns: Phase | Chapters | What happens | Key moment | What to watch for
* widths: 1 | 0.7 | 1.9 | 1.6 | 1.6



- **1. The Zoramite war** | Alma 43–44 | Zoramite apostates stir up the Lamanites; Zerahemnah invades. Moroni introduces body armor and consults the prophet Alma for intelligence, then traps the Lamanite army at the river Sidon. | A soldier scalps Zerahemnah mid-parley: “even as this scalp has fallen… so shall ye fall” (44:14). Lamanites who covenant peace are released unharmed. | The rules of Nephite warfare are established here: defend only, stop when the enemy covenants, prophets over spies.
- **2. The title of liberty** | Alma 45–46 | Alma departs; Amalickiah, seeking to be king, flatters the lower judges and church dissenters. Moroni rends his coat and rallies the people to covenant. | The title of liberty: “In memory of our God, our religion, and freedom, and our peace, our wives, and our children” (46:12). | The first “thus we see” of the war (46:8–10): the real enemy is forgetting, and “one very wicked man.”
- **3. Amalickiah’s rise** | Alma 47 | Fleeing to the Lamanites, Amalickiah gains their army by fraud — inviting Lehonti “down by degrees,” poisoning him, then murdering the Lamanite king and marrying the queen. | Poison “by degrees” (47:18) — the chapter is a parable of how dissent works, told as biography. | Mormon pauses the war for a full chapter of villain origin story. He wants the method understood.
- **4. Preparation vs. rage** | Alma 48–49 | Moroni fortifies every city while preparing the people’s hearts; Amalickiah’s armies attack Ammonihah and Noah and are bloodily repulsed by the new defenses. | “If all men had been… like unto Moroni, behold, the very powers of hell would have been shaken forever” (48:17). Amalickiah curses God (49:27). | Deliberately paired portraits — chapters 47 and 48 set Amalickiah’s rise and Moroni’s character side by side.
- **5. Fortification and fracture** | Alma 50–51 | New fortified cities rise; the Morianton land dispute; the king-men refuse to defend the country while Amalickiah storms the east coast, taking city after city. | Teancum slays Amalickiah in his tent on new year’s night, “a javelin to his heart” (51:34). | Every external loss follows an internal fracture — the east coast falls while the king-men fight Pahoran at home. Note 50:23: the “happiest time” verse sits mid-war.
- **6. Eastern theater: stratagems** | Alma 52–55 | Ammoron, Amalickiah’s brother, continues the war. Moroni, Teancum, and Lehi retake Mulek by decoy; the city of Gid is retaken by the wine stratagem, freeing prisoners without bloodshed. | Moroni’s men arm the freed prisoners inside the walls, and he refuses to fall upon drunken men (55:18–19) — victory without a battle. | Nephite stratagems consistently aim to avoid killing; compare every Lamanite stratagem in the same chapters.
- **7. Western theater: Helaman’s sons** | Alma 56–58 | Helaman’s epistle: the 2,060 stripling soldiers, the decoy at Antiparah, the taking of Cumeni and Manti — won while the army starves for want of supplies from home. | “Two hundred… had fainted because of the loss of blood; nevertheless… there was not one soul of them who did perish” (57:25). | The faith counterweight to phase 5: their mothers’ teaching versus the king-men’s treachery, running in the same years.
- **8. The home front and the end** | Alma 59–62 | Nephihah falls; Moroni writes his blistering — and mistaken — accusation to Pahoran; Pahoran’s meek reply reveals the Pachus insurrection. Together they retake Zarahemla, then Nephihah; Teancum kills Ammoron and dies doing it; peace returns in the 31st year. | Pahoran: “In your epistle you have censured me, but it mattereth not; I am not angry, but do rejoice in the greatness of your heart” (61:9). | The war’s climax is a letter exchange, not a battle. The coda (62:41): the same war “hardened” many and “softened” many — the unit’s thesis in one verse.

## Schematic map of the two theaters

Relative positions are inferred from the text (Alma 22:27–34; 50:7–15; 51:26; 56:9, 14) — the east-coast city chain taken by Amalickiah, the southwest line defended by Helaman, the river Sidon rising near Manti and flowing past Zarahemla, and the narrow strip of wilderness dividing Nephite from Lamanite lands. This is a schematic of the book’s internal geography, not a real-world map, and distances are not to scale.

Red arrows: the Amalickiah/Ammoron offensives — up the east coast toward Bountiful, and against the Manti line in the southwest. Blue arrows: the Nephite counteroffensives — Moroni, Teancum, and Lehi retaking the coastal cities from Bountiful; Helaman’s campaign from Judea toward Manti; and Moroni’s march home to retake Zarahemla from Pachus.

How to read the unit

Mormon, himself a general watching his own civilization die, chose to give twenty chapters to this fourteen-year war while compressing whole centuries elsewhere into a verse. Read with that choice in mind, the structure of the unit becomes visible. Nearly every crisis originates with Nephite dissenters rather than Lamanites — Zoramite officers, Amalickiah, Morianton, the king-men, Ammoron, Pachus — and every external defeat follows an internal fracture. The battles are the symptom; dissent, oaths, and the inner condition of the people are the disease.

The unit is also built on deliberate pairings. Chapters 47 and 48 set Amalickiah’s fraudulent rise beside Moroni’s character portrait. The eastern theater of stratagems (52–55) runs parallel to the western theater of faith (56–58). The king-men’s treachery at home runs in the same years as the stripling soldiers’ preservation in the field. And the climax of the whole war is not a battle but an exchange of letters — Moroni’s wrongly-aimed anger and Pahoran’s refusal to take offense — which resolves the internal fracture that made the external war unwinnable.
