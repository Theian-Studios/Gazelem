## Gospel language before the Gospels

- **1 Nephi 10:8** // “…there standeth one among you whom ye know not; and he is mightier than I, whose shoe’s latchet I am not worthy to unloose.” // Lehi, c. 600 BC, prophesying of John the Baptist — quoting words John would speak six centuries later. | **John 1:26–27** // “…there standeth one among you, whom ye know not; He it is, who coming after me is preferred before me, whose shoe’s latchet I am not worthy to unloose.”
- **1 Nephi 22:25** // “And he gathereth his children from the four quarters of the earth; and he numbereth his sheep, and they know him; and there shall be one fold and one shepherd…” // Nephi, c. 570 BC. Jesus later quotes the saying of himself in 3 Nephi 15–16, but Nephi’s use is unattributed. | **John 10:14–16** // “I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine… and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd.”
- **Enos 1:27** // “…he will say unto me: Come unto me, ye blessed, there is a place prepared for you in the mansions of my Father.” // Enos, c. 420 BC. See also Ether 12:32–37. | **John 14:2–3** // “In my Father’s house are many mansions… I go to prepare a place for you.”
- **Helaman 12:26** // “…fulfilling the words which say: They that have done good shall have everlasting life; and they that have done evil shall have everlasting damnation.” // Mormon introduces the passage at verse 25 with “we read,” and here with “the words which say” — an explicit citation formula pointing to a text that, in the biblical record, had not yet been spoken. | **John 5:29** // “…they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of damnation.”
- **Helaman 5:8** // “…that ye may do these things to lay up for yourselves a treasure in heaven, yea, which is eternal, and which fadeth not away…” // Helaman, c. 30 BC — before the Sermon reached the Nephites. |

- **Alma 5:52** // “Behold, the ax is laid at the root of the // tree; therefore every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit shall be hewn down and cast into the fire…” Alma attributes the words to the Spirit: “the Holy One hath spoken it.” | 
- **Alma 40:23** // “…yea, even a hair of the head shall not be lost; but all things shall be restored to their proper and perfect frame.” | **Luke 21:18** // “But there shall not an hair of your head perish.”

## The prophet like unto Moses

- **3 Nephi 20:23–26** // “Behold, I am he of whom Moses spake… every soul who will not hear that prophet shall be cut off from among the people… ye are the children of the prophets… the covenant which the Father made with your fathers, saying unto Abraham: And in thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed. ## Other extended correspondences
Ether 12:6–22 Moroni’s catalogue of what was accomplished “by faith” — a chain of examples introduced by the same formula and concluding with the assertion that these obtained a witness only after the trial of their faith.

Alma 13:1–19 The high priesthood “after the order of the Son,” held by Melchizedek, king of Salem, described as without beginning of days or end of years, with Abraham paying tithes.

Jacob 5; 1 Nephi 15:12–16 Israel as an olive tree; natural branches broken off, wild branches grafted in, natural branches grafted back in the last days.

Mosiah 27:8–24; Alma 36:6–24 Alma the Younger, going about to destroy the church, is stopped by an angel whose voice shakes the earth; he falls to the ground, is struck dumb and helpless for three days and nights, and rises converted and preaching. Companions hear the voice but do not understand.

4 Nephi 1:2–3, 15–17 After the Lord’s visit the people have all things common, no rich and poor, no contentions, no manner of –ites among them.

Mormon 9:22–25

Moroni quotes the Lord’s commission to the disciples: go into all the world, preach to every creature, he that believeth and is baptized shall be saved, and these signs shall follow — casting out devils, new tongues, taking up serpents, drinking deadly things unharmed, healing by the laying on of hands.

Moroni 4–5 The sacramental prayers over bread and wine, with the covenant to always remember him and keep his commandments, that they may always have his Spirit to be with them.

## Observations
• The parallels are unevenly distributed. Hebrews, 1 Corinthians, and the Johannine literature account for a large majority; the Pastoral Epistles and the shorter general epistles contribute almost nothing.

• Concentration follows the doctrinal sermons. Passages of narrative, warfare, migration, and political history in the Book of Mormon show few parallels; the density rises sharply in discourses on faith, resurrection, atonement, and the nature of God — exactly the subjects on which the New Testament epistles supply fixed English formulations.

• Several parallels appear in the mouths of speakers separated by centuries. “Death swallowed up” language occurs in Abinadi (c. 148 BC), Aaron (Alma 22, c. 90 BC), and Mormon (Mormon 7). “Garments washed white in the blood of

the Lamb” appears from 1 Nephi through Ether. This internal consistency is read as authorial habit on one account and as coherent prophetic vocabulary on the other.

• The strongest single case for verbal dependence is not any one phrase but the charity passage of Moroni 7:45–47, which reproduces eleven of Paul’s thirteen attributes in Paul’s order and wording — and then adds a definition, “the pure love of Christ,” that Paul does not supply.

• The chronologically sharpest case is 2 Nephi 25:13, where Nephi uses Malachi’s “healing in his wings” more than a century before Malachi wrote, and in a book the Book of Mormon itself says the Nephites did not possess until the risen Christ delivered it in 3 Nephi 24–25.

• The Book of Mormon marks quotation carefully elsewhere — Isaiah, Malachi, and the Sermon are introduced explicitly — which makes the unmarked cases a distinct category rather than an oversight. Two of them, Mormon 9:9 and Helaman 12:26, use citation language (“do we not read,” “the words which say”) for material that has no antecedent in the record they possessed.

• The textual data is identical under both models. What differs is the account of how the words got there — and, correspondingly, what a reader is entitled to conclude from a match.
