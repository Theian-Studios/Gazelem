# Name Changes & Their Meanings
* subtitle: When a new name marked a new man — covenant, circumstance, and conquest
* volume: old-testament
* order: 9
* columns: Original Name | New Name | Given By | The Meaning of the Change | Reference
* widths: 0.9 | 0.9 | 0.9 | 2.2 | 1

In the Old Testament a name is never merely a label; it is a statement of identity and destiny. So when God — or a king, or grief itself — gave a person a new name, something had genuinely changed. This chart sets the old name beside the new, with the meaning of each, and asks what the change was meant to say.

The covenant renamings are the heart of it: Abram becomes Abraham at the founding of a nation, Jacob becomes Israel after a night of wrestling, Hoshea becomes Joshua as he is set apart. Against these stand the names imposed by foreign kings on captives — a quieter kind of conquest, an attempt to write Babylon over Judah. Meanings follow standard Hebrew sources.

Fifteen renamings in three groups. Note the reversals of intent: the covenant names add the divine name (Hoshea → Joshua) or announce a promise, while the captivity names strip the divine name out (Azariah, “the Lord hath helped,” becomes Abed-nego). Meanings of Hebrew names are given as commonly rendered; several admit more than one derivation. The New Testament continues the pattern — Simon becomes Peter, Saul becomes Paul — and Revelation promises the faithful “a new name” (Revelation 2:17).

## The Covenant Renamings
New names given by God, at turning points of the covenant.

- Abram — “exalted father” | Abraham — “father of a multitude” | God | The promise made flesh: father of many nations | Gen 17:5
- Sarai — “my princess” | Sarah — “princess” | God | No longer of one house, but mother of nations and kings | Gen 17:15–16
- Jacob — “supplanter, heel-catcher” | Israel — “he prevails with God” | The Lord, at Peniel | The deceiver becomes the one who has power with God | Gen 32:28; 35:10
- Hoshea — “salvation” | Joshua — “the Lord is salvation” | Moses | The Lord’s name written into the deliverer’s own | Num 13:16

## Names Born of Circumstance
Names that a moment of joy, grief, or bitterness pressed upon a life.

- Ben-oni — “son of my sorrow” | Benjamin — “son of the right hand” | Jacob (over Rachel’s word) | A father recasts a mother’s dying grief as favor | Gen 35:18
- Esau — “hairy” | Edom — “red” | By the red pottage | Named for the appetite that cost him his birthright | Gen 25:30
- Naomi — “pleasant” | Mara — “bitter” | Herself, in mourning | “Call me not Naomi… the Almighty hath dealt bitterly” | Ruth 1:20
- Solomon — “peaceable” | Jedidiah — “beloved of the Lord” | The Lord, through Nathan | A sign of divine favor upon David’s son | 2 Sam 12:24–25
- Gideon — “hewer” | Jerubbaal — “let Baal contend” | His father Joash | Named for tearing down Baal’s altar | Judg 6:32

## Names Imposed in Captivity
The foreign names laid on Judah’s sons by kings who ruled them.

- Daniel — “God is my judge” | Belteshazzar | The prince of the eunuchs | A Babylonian name honoring the god Bel | Dan 1:7
- Hananiah — “the Lord is gracious” | Shadrach | The prince of the eunuchs | A Babylonian renaming of the three companions | Dan 1:7
- Mishael — “who is what God is” | Meshach | The prince of the eunuchs | Israel’s God erased from the name | Dan 1:7
- Azariah — “the Lord hath helped” | Abed-nego | The prince of the eunuchs | Renamed for a Babylonian deity | Dan 1:7
- Eliakim — “God will establish” | Jehoiakim | Pharaoh-nechoh | A vassal king renamed by his overlord | 2 Kgs 23:34
- Mattaniah — “gift of the Lord” | Zedekiah — “righteousness of the Lord” | Nebuchadnezzar | The last king of Judah renamed by his conqueror | 2 Kgs 24:17
