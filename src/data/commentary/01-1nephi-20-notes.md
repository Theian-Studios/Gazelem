Chapter context: 1 Nephi 20 is Nephi's transcription of Isaiah 48 — the first
chapter-length Isaiah block in the volume, read to his brothers so that he
might, in his own words, more fully persuade them. The phrase level treats
the block's variants from the King James text, which are unusually thick in
this chapter.

## WORLD BEHIND THE TEXT

1. **The reading frame.** The previous chapter sets the scene: Nephi reads
   from the brass plates to persuade his brothers to believe in their
   Redeemer, and instructs them to liken the words to themselves
   (19:23–24). Isaiah 48 is his opening selection — a chapter addressed to
   a covenant people in exile who name the name but not the faith, which is
   precisely his diagnosis of Laman and Lemuel.
2. **Babylon on the horizon.** The chapter Isaiah wrote speaks to Judah
   facing Babylon — 20:14 w43–49 "his arm shall come upon the Chaldeans",
   20:20 w1–5 "Go ye forth of Babylon". Nephi's family has just escaped the
   Jerusalem that Babylon will destroy; the exile text lands on actual
   exiles reading it in a new hemisphere.
3. **Brass-plates text.** This chapter diverges from the King James text
   more often and more substantively than most quoted blocks (compare the
   near-verbatim Mosiah 14). Whether the differences reflect the brass
   plates or the translation cannot be settled verse by verse; the phrase
   notes flag the readings and what each does.
4. **Water language for a desert people.** The chapter's covenant imagery
   runs on water — 20:1 w21–26 "out of the waters of Judah",
   20:21 w5–10 "he led them through the deserts", waters from the rock — read
   aloud by a man whose family crossed deserts on the strength of divine
   direction.

## DEPTH: WORD LEVEL

### "hearken" (vv. 1, 12, 14, 16, 18)
The chapter's drumbeat — five occurrences, opening the block
(20:1 w1–4 "Hearken and hear this"), summoning Jacob
(20:12 w1–5 "Hearken unto me, O Jacob"), convening the nations (v. 14),
commanding approach (v. 16), and grounding the great conditional
(20:18 w1–8 "O that thou hadst hearkened to my commandments—then"). The verb asks
for more than hearing; the tragedy of v. 18 is that the condition is
past-tense.

### "obstinate" (v. 4)
20:4 w5–11 "because I knew that thou art obstinate" — the Lord's stated reason
for prophecy's existence: foreknowledge was given *because* of stubbornness,
so that no one could credit an idol afterward (v. 5). The word's anatomy is
spelled out in the same verse: 20:4 w13–22 "thy neck is an iron sinew, and thy brow brass"
— stiffness of neck and hardness of face, the body language of refusal.

### "refined" (v. 10)
20:10 w3–6 "I have refined thee" — smelting language: affliction as furnace,
covenant people as ore. This text sharpens the claim by omission (see the
phrase note on the dropped "but not with silver") and by the chosen site:
20:10 w12–15 "the furnace of affliction", election located inside the suffering,
not despite it.

### "pollute" (v. 11)
20:11 w15–23 "I will not suffer my name to be polluted" — defilement language
applied to reputation: the Lord acts for his own sake because deferral of
judgment could be misread as weakness or absence. The word makes divine
patience a name-management problem, which the chapter's whole argument
(prophecy given so idols get no credit, vv. 4–5) turns on.

## DEPTH: PHRASE LEVEL

### "or out of the waters of baptism" (v. 1)
The block's most famous plus: the King James text has Israel come forth
"out of the waters of Judah" only; this text adds the gloss
20:1 w27–33 "or out of the waters of baptism", converting a genealogical image
into a covenant-ordinance one. (The gloss is a known latecomer to the
printed Book of Mormon text, entering in the 1840 edition — a printing-
history note, not a source-text claim.) Functionally it reads v. 1's
hypocrisy charge through the ordinance: baptized people who
20:1 w35–41 "swear by the name of the Lord" without truth.

### "but they do not stay themselves upon the God of Israel" (v. 2)
The chapter's sharpest variant: the King James text says the people call
themselves of the holy city *and stay themselves* upon the God of Israel —
this text negates the clause. What the KJV leaves as implicit irony
becomes explicit indictment: self-designation without reliance. For
Nephi's purpose — brothers who invoke the Lord's name against him — the
negated reading is the sermon.

### The dropped "but not with silver" (v. 10)
A subtraction: the King James "I have refined thee, but not with silver"
loses its qualifier here, leaving 20:10 w2–6 "behold, I have refined thee"
absolute. Where the KJV hedges the image (refined, though not as
silver is), this text lets the image stand whole — with the modernized
spellings elsewhere in the chapter (showed for shewed, shouldst for
shouldest), part of the evidence that the block has been smoothed in
transmission or translation rather than copied mechanically.

### "for I will not suffer my name to be polluted" (v. 11)
Where the King James text asks a rhetorical question ("for how should my
name be polluted?"), this text answers it as resolve. Question becomes
declaration — the same direction of change as the v. 2 variant, replacing
an implication with a commitment.

### "yea, and he will fulfil his word which he hath declared by them" (v. 14)
An addition without any King James counterpart: the verse's claim that the
Lord loves the agent of Babylon's fall gains a clause insisting that the
word declared *by the prophets* will be fulfilled. The insertion matches
the chapter's running argument (prediction as proof, vv. 3–7) and Nephi's
use of it: he is at that moment fulfilling the role of the declaring
prophet his brothers doubt.

### "And notwithstanding he hath done all this, and greater also" (v. 22)
The closing variant: the King James chapter ends bluntly — no peace to the
wicked. This text prefixes the sentence with a ledger of mercy:
20:22 w2–10 "notwithstanding he hath done all this, and greater also", there
is still no peace to the wicked. Judgment is framed as arriving *despite*
accumulated kindness — the exact shape of the chapter's whole argument,
and of Nephi's family history as he reads it.

## DEPTH: VERSE LEVEL

**(vv. 1–2)** The summons and the charge. The block opens by naming its
audience four ways — house of Jacob, called by Israel's name, come from
Judah's waters (and baptism's), swearers by the Lord's name — and then
voids the credentials: 20:1 w50–58 "yet they swear not in truth nor in righteousness",
20:2 w10–19 "they do not stay themselves upon the God of Israel". Identity
without reliance is the whole indictment, delivered before any history is
reviewed.

**(vv. 3–8)** The argument from prediction. Former things
20:3 w4 "declared" in advance and performed suddenly, *because*
(vv. 4–5) obstinacy would otherwise credit idols; new things
20:7 w3–9 "created now, and not from the beginning", withheld until this moment
so that no one can say 20:7 w27–30 "say—Behold I knew them". Verse 8 closes the trap: the audience neither
heard nor knew, 20:8 w20–27 "I knew that thou wouldst deal very treacherously"
— foreknowledge of treachery, and the title
20:8 w31–35 "a transgressor from the womb".

**(vv. 9–11)** Mercy for the name's sake. Three first-person resolutions:
anger deferred 20:9 w2–5 "for my name's sake", the refining election of
v. 10, and the double 20:11 w1–4 "For mine own sake" of v. 11 — the Lord's
fidelity anchored in his own character precisely because the people's
provides no anchor. The furnace verse sits at the center: affliction as
the *site* of choosing, the verse Nephi's family can read
autobiographically.

**(vv. 12–16)** The self-declaration. The summoned assembly hears the
credentials: 20:13 w1–10 "Mine hand hath also laid the foundation of the earth",
sovereignty over Babylon's fall (v. 14), and the claim that closes the
section — 20:16 w6–14 "I have not spoken in secret; from the beginning" —
prophecy as public record, verifiable, the opposite of the idols'
silence. Verse 16 ends with a voice shift ("the Lord God, and his Spirit,
hath sent me") that reads as the messenger stepping forward.

**(vv. 17–19)** The great conditional. The Redeemer teaches
20:17 w30–34 "the way thou shouldst go", and then the lament:
20:18 w1–15 "O that thou hadst hearkened to my commandments—then had thy peace been as a river"
— peace, righteousness, posterity, all in the past-perfect of what would
have been. The what-might-have-been is the tenderest verse in the chapter and
the cruelest: the blessings are described in full, as forfeited.

**(vv. 20–22)** Exodus and the exception. The command to flee Babylon
20:20 w11–15 "with a voice of singing", the desert-provision memory
(20:21 w23–32 "he clave the rock also and the waters gushed out"), and the
close: after all this and greater, 20:22 w11–20 "there is no peace, saith the Lord, unto the wicked".
The chapter ends where Nephi's brothers stand — between an exodus already
granted and a peace still conditional.

## DEPTH: CHAPTER LEVEL

1. **A courtroom in three movements.** Indictment (vv. 1–8: credentials
   voided, treachery foreknown), character witness (vv. 9–16: the Lord's
   own record — prediction, creation, public speech), verdict deferred
   (vv. 17–22: the conditional peace). The block argues like a trial in
   which the judge testifies about himself because the defendants'
   testimony is worthless.
2. **Prediction as the argument.** The chapter's internal logic — former
   things declared before they happened so idols get no credit — is also
   the reason Nephi selected it: he has just told his brothers that he
   reads scripture to persuade them of a Redeemer they have not seen.
   Isaiah's way of proving (announce in advance, then perform) is
   Nephi's method.
3. **Variant density.** This chapter departs from the King James text far
   more than the volume's other early blocks — additions (vv. 1, 14, 22),
   a negation (v. 2), a question flattened to resolve (v. 11), a
   subtraction (v. 10). The variants trend one direction: implication
   becomes statement, irony becomes indictment, hedges drop. The block as
   this volume carries it is a *harder* Isaiah 48 than the KJV's.
4. **Structure:** the chapter is stitched by the fivefold "hearken" (vv. 1,
   12, 14, 16, 18) — summons, summons, assembly, approach, lament — a
   listening-command that escalates until it collapses into the
   past-conditional of v. 18.

## DEPTH: BOOK LEVEL

1. **First block of the Isaiah program.** Chapters 20–21 open the practice
   that will dominate 2 Nephi: wholesale Isaiah, framed by likening
   (19:23) and followed by exposition (22). Within 1 Nephi the two
   chapters are the proof-text for everything Nephi has claimed about
   scattering, covenant, and a Redeemer — argued now from the national
   scripture rather than his own visions.
2. **Aimed at Laman and Lemuel.** The book's running conflict gives the
   block its edge: brothers who swear by the Lord's name against Nephi
   (17:48, 55) hear a chapter about swearers not in truth; men who have
   seen an angel and forgotten hear about obstinacy that prophecy was
   designed to outflank. Chapter 22 confirms they heard it as aimed —
   their first question is what it means.
3. **The furnace before the furnace.** Verse 10's election-in-affliction
   reads prospectively within the book: the family's desert years are
   behind, but the small plates are written knowing the nation's
   afflictions ahead. Nephi's later resumption of Isaiah in 2 Nephi keeps
   returning to this chapter's claim that chastening is covenant
   procedure, not covenant failure.
4. **The exodus template.** Verses 20–21 retell the first exodus at the
   moment Nephi's family has completed a second one; the book has already
   drawn the parallel explicitly (17:23–44, Nephi's own Moses argument).
   The quoted chapter lets Isaiah make Nephi's favorite argument for him.

## WORLD IN FRONT OF THE TEXT

### Applications

**Credentials versus reliance (vv. 1–2).** The block's opening distinguishes
naming the name from staying oneself upon God — membership language from
weight-bearing trust. The audit it invites: which of my religious
identifiers would survive the v. 2 negation test, and what am I actually
leaning on when things load-bear?

**Reading the furnace as election (v. 10).** The verse offers a frame for
affliction that neither denies it nor explains it away: chosen *in* the
furnace. Applied, it changes the question asked inside hard seasons from
"why is this happening" to "what is being refined."

### Self-reflection questions

- (v. 1) Where do I swear by the name — invoke the identity — without the
  truth-and-righteousness that the verse says makes it real?
- (v. 18) What is my version of the past-conditional — the commandment I
  did not hearken to, whose river-peace I can now only read about — and
  what does v. 22's "notwithstanding" mercy make possible from here?
- (vv. 3–7) What has God told me in advance, precisely so that when it
  happened I could not credit my own idols?
