# How the Book of Mormon Came Forth

People who changed how the Book of Mormon is translated, printed, edited, defended, or read — each set at the year of the work they are remembered for.

---

# 1827 · Joseph Smith

* years: 1805–1844
* role: Translator
* image: joseph-smith

Translator of the record and the source of nearly everything we know about its coming forth. His late-life uses of the book — calling it "the most correct of any book on earth" and "the keystone of our religion" (Nov. 28, 1841; Introduction) — set the frame every later era returned to. His 1837 and 1840 editions established that the text could be editorially revised by prophetic authority, a fact foundational to all later textual criticism.

# 1828 · Emma Smith

* years: 1804–1879
* role: Scribe
* image: emma-smith

Scribe during the earliest phase of the translation in Harmony and guardian of the manuscript in the years of danger. Her late-life testimony to her son remains one of the key eyewitness accounts of the dictation: Joseph translating "hour after hour with nothing between us," spelling out names he could not pronounce, never asking to have a passage read back — an account foundational to every modern study of the translation process.

# 1829 · Oliver Cowdery

* years: 1806–1850
* role: Principal scribe
* image: oliver-cowdery

Principal scribe of nearly the entire original manuscript, first baptized witness of the translation process, and author of the earliest published accounts of Moroni's visits (1834–35 letters in the *Messenger and Advocate*). The documentary trail of the translation runs through his pen.

# 1829 · Martin Harris

* years: 1783–1875
* role: Financier · Witness
* image: martin-harris

The book's financier and first evangelist of its authenticity. He carried the transcribed characters to Charles Anthon (Feb. 1828), lost the 116 manuscript pages that reshaped the record's structure (June 1828; D&C 3, 10), stood as one of the Three Witnesses, and mortgaged his farm to pay E. B. Grandin's $3,000 printing bill — without which there is no 1830 edition.

# 1830 · John H. Gilbert

* years: 1802–1895
* role: Compositor
* image: john-gilbert

E. B. Grandin's compositor, who set the type of the 1830 edition from a printer's manuscript that arrived essentially unpunctuated — meaning Gilbert supplied virtually all of the first edition's punctuation and paragraphing himself. Every text-critical question about commas, sentence breaks, and early readings runs through his casework, and his detailed 1892 reminiscence is the primary source on the book's printing.

# 1879 · Orson Pratt

* years: 1811–1881
* role: Editor · Apologist
* image: orson-pratt

The book's first great systematic advocate and its most consequential early editor. His 1879 edition created the chapter and verse divisions still in use, added the first footnote apparatus, and embedded geographic identifications (later removed). His pamphlets — *Divine Authenticity of the Book of Mormon* (1850–51) — made the book the centerpiece of the Church's public argument for the Restoration.

# 1895 · B. H. Roberts

* years: 1857–1933
* role: Historian
* image: bh-roberts

The Church's first serious internal critic-believer. *New Witnesses for God* (1895–1909) built the era's most rigorous evidentiary case; his privately circulated *Studies of the Book of Mormon* (1920s) then confronted the hardest problems — View of the Hebrews parallels, anachronisms — with an honesty that set the agenda for a century of scholarship. He modeled the possibility of faithful criticism.

# 1920 · James E. Talmage

* years: 1862–1933
* role: Editor
* image: james-talmage

Chaired the committee for the 1920 edition: double columns, new punctuation, chapter summaries, pronunciation guide, and corrections against earlier editions — the physical form of the book for sixty years, and the Church's first systematic modern editorial pass.

# 1947 · Sidney B. Sperry

* years: 1895–1977
* role: Biblical scholar
* image: sidney-sperry

The first University of Chicago–trained biblical scholar to devote a career to the Book of Mormon at BYU. *Our Book of Mormon* (1947) and *Book of Mormon Compendium* (1968) brought genre analysis and Hebrew-literary questions into Latter-day Saint study — an early forerunner of the literary turn.

# 1950 · Hugh Nibley

* years: 1910–2005
* role: Philologist
* image: hugh-nibley

Transformed the field's ambitions. *Lehi in the Desert* (1950), *An Approach to the Book of Mormon* (1957), and *Since Cumorah* (1967) read the book against ancient Near Eastern languages, cultures, and texts, arguing it behaves like an authentic ancient document. He created the template — deep philology, comparative antiquity, combative wit — that a generation of scholars followed, and he made rigorous scholarship on the book respectable inside the Church.

# 1967 · John W. Welch

* years: b. 1946
* role: Founder of FARMS
* image: john-welch

Discovered chiasmus in the Book of Mormon as a missionary in 1967 — the single most influential literary finding in the book's history — and then built the institutions: founder of FARMS (1979), longtime editor of *BYU Studies*, organizer of the critical-text and legal-studies projects (*The Legal Cases in the Book of Mormon*, 2008). More than anyone, he professionalized the field.

# 1981 · Bruce R. McConkie

* years: 1915–1985
* role: Editor
* image: bruce-mcconkie

Wrote the chapter headings the whole Church read for forty years, and with Thomas S. Monson and Boyd K. Packer oversaw the Scriptures Publication Committee that produced the footnotes, Topical Guide integration, and the "Another Testament of Jesus Christ" subtitle (added 1982).

# 1985 · John L. Sorenson

* years: 1924–2021
* role: Anthropologist
* image: john-sorenson

*An Ancient American Setting for the Book of Mormon* (1985) made the limited Mesoamerican geography the working model of faithful scholarship, replacing hemispheric assumptions and reframing questions of population, culture, and "others" in the land. *Mormon's Codex* (2013) was his summa.

# 1986 · Ezra Taft Benson

* years: 1899–1994
* role: President of the Church
* image: ezra-taft-benson

No one changed the Church's relationship to the book more in the twentieth century. "The Book of Mormon — Keystone of Our Religion" (Oct. 1986) and "Flooding the Earth with the Book of Mormon" (Oct. 1988) declared the Church under condemnation for neglecting it (citing D&C 84:54–57) and moved it to the center of curriculum, missionary work, and personal study. The modern culture of daily Book of Mormon reading is largely his legacy.

# 1988 · Royal Skousen

* years: b. 1945
* role: Textual critic
* image: royal-skousen

The Critical Text Project (1988–present): definitive transcriptions of the original and printer's manuscripts, the six-volume *Analysis of Textual Variants*, *The Earliest Text* (Yale, 2009), and the *History of the Text* volumes. Recovered hundreds of original readings, documented the Early Modern English profile of the dictation, and gave the book the full text-critical apparatus of a classical text — arguably the most important scholarly work ever done on it.

# 2005 · Gordon B. Hinckley

* years: 1910–2008
* role: President of the Church
* image: gordon-hinckley

His August 2005 challenge to read or reread the entire book by year's end produced the largest coordinated reading of the text in history and reinforced the Benson-era centering.

# 2017 · Russell M. Nelson

* years: 1924–2025
* role: President of the Church
* image: russell-nelson

"The Book of Mormon: What Would Your Life Be Like without It?" (Oct. 2017) issued the whole-book reading challenge anew, tying the book to his ministry themes of revelation and covenant; he also presided over the 2018 curriculum shift (*Come, Follow Me*) that made home-centered scripture study — with the Book of Mormon on a recurring four-year cycle — the Church's teaching backbone.
