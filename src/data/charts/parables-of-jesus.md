# The Parables of Jesus Christ
* subtitle: Audience, occasion, and interpretation — across the four Gospels
* volume: new-testament
* order: 4
* columns: Parable | Audience & Occasion | Interpreted in the Text? | Reference (KJV)
* widths: 1.4 | 2 | 1 | 1.2

Each entry records who was listening, what provoked the parable, and whether Jesus interprets it in the text — a smaller set than most readers assume. Two patterns surface: Luke’s unique parables cluster around mercy and money, and the hardest confrontation parables are aimed at religious insiders, not crowds. Synoptic parallels are combined in single entries with references in parentheses.

41 parables in 6 groups. “Interpreted” distinguishes full allegorical explanations (only the Sower and the Wheat and Tares receive them) from stated applications and closing sayings. Classification is interpretive at the margins — the Sheep and the Goats is an enacted judgment scene, and several sayings (lamp, salt, physician) are aphorisms rather than narrative parables and are not listed.

## Parables of the Kingdom
The Matthew 13 / Mark 4 cycle — what the kingdom of heaven is like.

- The Sower | The multitude by the sea; explained privately to the disciples | Yes — fully (Matt 13:18–23) | Matt 13:3–9 (Mk 4:3–9; Lk 8:5–8)
- The Wheat and the Tares | The multitude; explained in the house to the disciples | Yes — fully (Matt 13:36–43) | Matt 13:24–30
- The Mustard Seed | The multitude | No | Matt 13:31–32 (Mk 4:30–32; Lk 13:18–19)
- The Leaven | The multitude | No | Matt 13:33 (Lk 13:20–21)
- The Seed Growing Secretly | The multitude — unique to Mark | No | Mark 4:26–29
- The Hidden Treasure | The disciples, in the house | No | Matt 13:44
- The Pearl of Great Price | The disciples | No | Matt 13:45–46
- The Net | The disciples | Yes — briefly (Matt 13:49–50) | Matt 13:47–50
- The Householder’s Treasure, New and Old | The disciples — the scribe instructed unto the kingdom | No | Matt 13:52

## Parables of Mercy and the Lost
Luke’s great cluster — provoked by murmuring at the company He kept.

- The Lost Sheep | Pharisees and scribes murmuring that He received sinners; in Matthew, to the disciples concerning little ones | No — application stated (Lk 15:7) | Luke 15:4–7 (Matt 18:12–14)
- The Lost Coin | Same occasion | No — application stated (Lk 15:10) | Luke 15:8–10
- The Prodigal Son | Same occasion — the elder brother answers the murmurers | No | Luke 15:11–32
- The Good Samaritan | A lawyer asking “Who is my neighbour?” | No — turned into a command (Lk 10:36–37) | Luke 10:30–37
- The Two Debtors | Simon the Pharisee, judging the woman who washed His feet | Yes — applied to Simon (Lk 7:44–47) | Luke 7:41–43
- The Unmerciful Servant | Peter, after “how oft shall my brother sin against me?” | Yes — application stated (Matt 18:35) | Matt 18:23–35
- The Pharisee and the Publican | “certain which trusted in themselves that they were righteous” | Yes — application stated (Lk 18:14) | Luke 18:9–14

## Parables of Money and Stewardship
Luke’s economics of the kingdom, and Matthew’s accounting of talents.

- The Rich Fool | One who wanted his inheritance divided; the covetous crowd | Yes — application stated (Lk 12:21) | Luke 12:16–21
- The Unjust Steward | The disciples, with Pharisees listening | Yes — applications stated (Lk 16:8–13) | Luke 16:1–13
- The Rich Man and Lazarus | The covetous Pharisees who derided Him | No | Luke 16:19–31
- The Labourers in the Vineyard | The disciples, after “we have forsaken all” | No — closing saying (Matt 20:16) | Matt 20:1–16
- The Talents | The disciples, on Olivet | No — judgment enacted within | Matt 25:14–30
- The Pounds | The disciples nearing Jerusalem, “because they thought the kingdom of God should immediately appear” | No | Luke 19:12–27
- Counting the Cost — the Tower and the Warring King | Great multitudes following Him | Yes — application stated (Lk 14:33) | Luke 14:28–33

## Parables of Prayer
Persistence before God, taught by contrast.

- The Friend at Midnight | The disciples, after teaching the Lord’s Prayer | Yes — application stated (Lk 11:9–13) | Luke 11:5–8
- The Importunate Widow and the Unjust Judge | The disciples, “that men ought always to pray, and not to faint” | Yes — purpose stated first (Lk 18:1, 6–8) | Luke 18:1–8

## Parables of Israel, the Vineyard, and Rejection
The confrontation parables — most aimed at religious insiders in the temple.

- The Two Builders — the House on the Rock | Closing the Sermon on the Mount | Yes — built into the telling | Matt 7:24–27 (Lk 6:47–49)
- The Children in the Marketplace | The crowd, concerning that generation’s response to John and to Him | Yes — applied (Matt 11:18–19) | Matt 11:16–19 (Lk 7:31–35)
- New Cloth and New Wine in Old Bottles | John’s disciples and Pharisees, on fasting | No | Matt 9:16–17 (Mk 2:21–22; Lk 5:36–39)
- The Barren Fig Tree | The crowd, after news of Pilate’s slaughter — a stay of judgment | No | Luke 13:6–9
- The Great Supper | Guests at a chief Pharisee’s table | No | Luke 14:16–24
- The Two Sons | Chief priests and elders challenging His authority, in the temple | Yes — applied to them (Matt 21:31–32) | Matt 21:28–32
- The Wicked Husbandmen | Chief priests, elders, and Pharisees — “they perceived that he spake of them”; the JST adds an extended interpretive frame | Yes — applied (Matt 21:42–45) | Matt 21:33–46 (Mk 12:1–12; Lk 20:9–19; JST Matt 21)
- The Marriage of the King’s Son | Same temple audience | No — closing saying (Matt 22:14) | Matt 22:1–14
- The Chief Rooms and the Lowest Room | Guests jockeying for place at the Pharisee’s feast | Yes — application stated (Lk 14:11) | Luke 14:7–11

## Parables of Watchfulness and Judgment
The Olivet cycle — readiness for a returning Lord.

- The Fig Tree Budding | Peter, James, John, and Andrew on Olivet | Yes — application stated (Matt 24:33) | Matt 24:32–33 (Mk 13:28–29; Lk 21:29–31)
- The Thief in the Night | The disciples on Olivet | Yes — application stated (Matt 24:44) | Matt 24:43–44 (Lk 12:39–40)
- The Faithful and Wise Servant | Peter’s question, “speakest thou this parable unto us, or even to all?” | Yes — built into the telling | Matt 24:45–51 (Lk 12:42–48)
- The Watching Servants and the Porter | The disciples — “what I say unto you I say unto all, Watch” | Yes — application stated (Mk 13:37) | Mark 13:34–37 (Lk 12:35–38)
- The Ten Virgins | The disciples on Olivet | Yes — application stated (Matt 25:13) | Matt 25:1–13
- The Sheep and the Goats | The disciples on Olivet — an enacted judgment scene rather than a strict parable | Yes — built into the telling | Matt 25:31–46
- The Unprofitable Servants | The apostles, after “Increase our faith” | Yes — application stated (Lk 17:10) | Luke 17:7–10
