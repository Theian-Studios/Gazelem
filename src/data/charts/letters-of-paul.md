Paul never wrote into a vacuum: every letter answers a specific occasion. Read the “why” column and the epistles become replies rather than essays — Galatians exists because of the Judaizers, 1 Corinthians because of a report and a letter, Philemon because of one runaway slave. Dates and places of writing follow the traditional reckoning; several are debated by scholars, as noted below.

14 epistles in 4 groups. Dates and places of writing are the traditional estimates; the dating of Galatians (and whether it is Paul’s earliest letter), the destination of Ephesians, and above all the authorship of Hebrews (which is anonymous, and is ascribed to Paul by long tradition but questioned by many) are all matters of scholarly debate. This chart gives the traditional framing; a reader may weigh the alternatives.

## The Missionary-Journey Epistles
Written on the road, into live crises in churches Paul had planted.

- 1 Thessalonians | Corinth, c. AD 51 | The new Gentile church at Thessalonica | Timothy’s report after Paul was driven out; the believers grieved over those who had died before Christ’s return | Comfort and hope — the dead in Christ shall rise; live holy while you wait
- 2 Thessalonians | Corinth, c. AD 51–52 | The same church | Some had grown idle, thinking the day of the Lord had already come | The man of sin must come first; stand fast, and work quietly
- Galatians | c. AD 49–55 (debated) | The churches of Galatia | Judaizers pressing circumcision and the law of Moses upon Gentile converts | Justified by faith in Christ, not works of the law; Christian liberty and the fruit of the Spirit
- 1 Corinthians | Ephesus, c. AD 55 | The divided church at Corinth | A report from Chloe’s household of factions, plus a letter of questions on marriage, idols, gifts, and the resurrection | Unity in Christ, holiness, charity (ch. 13), and the resurrection (ch. 15)
- 2 Corinthians | Macedonia, c. AD 55–56 | The same church | “False apostles” attacking Paul’s authority; the aftermath of a painful letter; the collection for Jerusalem | The ministry of reconciliation; strength made perfect in weakness; cheerful giving
- Romans | Corinth, c. AD 57 | The church at Rome, which he had not yet visited | To prepare a church he did not found for his coming, and to enlist support for Spain | His most systematic gospel — righteousness by faith for Jew and Gentile alike

## The Prison Epistles
Written from confinement, most likely in Rome (c. AD 60–62).

- Ephesians | Rome (prison), c. AD 60–62 | The Ephesians — likely a circular letter | No crisis — a serene meditation on the church | The uniting of all things in Christ; one body of Jew and Gentile; the whole armour of God
- Philippians | Rome (prison), c. AD 61–62 | The beloved church at Philippi | Thanks for their gift sent by Epaphroditus — a letter of joy from a jail | Rejoice in the Lord always; the mind of Christ, who humbled Himself (ch. 2)
- Colossians | Rome (prison), c. AD 60–62 | The church at Colosse | A “philosophy” of angel-worship and asceticism was displacing Christ | The pre-eminence and all-sufficiency of Christ, in whom dwells all the fulness
- Philemon | Rome (prison), c. AD 60–62 | Philemon, a Christian slaveholder at Colosse | Onesimus, Philemon’s runaway slave, was converted by Paul and sent home | Receive him “not now as a servant, but... a brother beloved” — the gospel remaking a bond

## The Pastoral Epistles
Written to two trusted delegates, on ordering the churches.

- 1 Timothy | Macedonia (?), c. AD 62–64 | Timothy, left in charge at Ephesus | False teachers and disorder in the congregation | How to behave in the house of God; the qualifications of bishops and deacons
- Titus | c. AD 62–64 | Titus, left on the island of Crete | The Cretan churches needed ordering and elders appointed | Sound doctrine adorned by good works; grace that teaches godly living
- 2 Timothy | Rome (prison), c. AD 64–67 | Timothy — Paul’s last letter | Awaiting execution; “Demas hath forsaken me”; only Luke remained | The final charge — “preach the word”; endure; “I have fought a good fight”

## To the Hebrews
Traditionally grouped with Paul, though the epistle itself is anonymous.

- Hebrews | c. AD 60s | Jewish Christians tempted to fall back into Judaism | Persecution and discouragement drawing them back to the old covenant | Christ’s superiority to angels, Moses, and Aaron — the great High Priest after the order of Melchizedek; hold fast by faith (ch. 11)
