# The Sermons of Acts
* subtitle: The apostolic preaching — preacher, audience, scripture, and outcome
* volume: new-testament
* order: 13
* columns: Preacher | Occasion | Audience | Scriptures & Themes Marshaled | The Outcome | Reference (KJV)

Acts is a book of sermons: the same gospel proclaimed again and again, flexing to fit each audience. Before Jews the apostles reason from the law and the prophets; before pagans at Lystra and Athens they begin instead from the God of creation. Read the outcome column and the range appears — from three thousand baptized to a martyr stoned to “we will hear thee again.”

Ten sermons in one view. A note on method: to synagogue audiences the apostles argue from shared scripture, but at Lystra and Athens — where there was no shared Bible — they begin from creation and conscience, reaching the same Christ by another road. Scripture citations follow the King James Version.

- Peter | At Pentecost | The multitude of Jews from every nation | Joel 2 (the Spirit poured out); Ps 16 and Ps 110 (the risen, enthroned Christ) | About 3,000 souls baptized | Acts 2:14–41
- Peter | At Solomon’s Porch | The people, amazed at the healed lame man | Deut 18 (a prophet like Moses); the covenant to Abraham | Many believed — the men about 5,000; Peter and John seized | Acts 3:12–26
- Peter | Before the Sanhedrin | The rulers, elders, and scribes | Ps 118 (the stone the builders rejected) | “None other name... whereby we must be saved” | Acts 4:8–12
- Peter | At Cornelius’ House | Cornelius and his household — Gentiles | “God is no respecter of persons”; the witness of the prophets | The Holy Ghost fell on the Gentiles; they were baptized | Acts 10:34–48
- Stephen | Before the Council | The Sanhedrin | Israel’s whole history — Abraham, Joseph, Moses; Amos 5 and Isaiah 66 | Stoned to death — the first Christian martyr | Acts 7:2–60
- Paul | At Pisidian Antioch | Jews and God-fearing Gentiles in the synagogue | Ps 2 and Ps 16 (resurrection); Isa 55; Hab 1 | Many followed; the whole city gathered next sabbath; opposition stirred | Acts 13:16–41
- Paul & Barnabas | At Lystra | Pagan Lycaonians, who took them for gods | The living God who gives rain and fruitful seasons — natural revelation | They scarcely restrained the crowd from sacrificing to them | Acts 14:15–17
- Paul | At Athens (the Areopagus) | Stoic and Epicurean philosophers | “The Unknown God”; a Greek poet — “we are also his offspring” | Some mocked; some said “We will hear thee again”; a few believed | Acts 17:22–34
- Paul | At Miletus (Farewell) | The elders of the church at Ephesus | “All the counsel of God”; “It is more blessed to give than to receive” | A weeping farewell — they would see his face no more | Acts 20:18–35
- Paul | Before Agrippa | King Agrippa and Festus | His own conversion; Moses and the prophets | “Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian” | Acts 26:2–29
