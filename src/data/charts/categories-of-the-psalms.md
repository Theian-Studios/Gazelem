# The Categories of the Psalms
* subtitle: The many kinds of song in Israel’s hymnbook — and how to tell them apart
* volume: old-testament
* order: 5
* columns: The Category | What Marks It | Representative Psalms
* widths: 1 | 2 | 1.2

The book of Psalms is not one thing but many — one hundred and fifty poems written across centuries, for temple and battlefield, coronation and captivity, private grief and national joy. Sorting them by type turns a long book into a usable one: when you know what kind of psalm you are reading, you know what it is trying to do. This chart lays out the great categories with their marks and representative examples.

The types overlap freely — a single psalm may lament, then trust, then praise, all in a dozen verses, and many belong to more than one column below. The classification is a lens, not a cage. Numbers follow the King James Psalter.

Sixteen categories in three groups. The scheme blends the classic form-critical types (hymn, lament, thanksgiving, royal) with thematic groupings long used by readers (Zion, creation, penitential, ascents). Assignments are not exclusive — Psalm 19 is at once a creation psalm and a Torah psalm; Psalm 22 is both an individual lament and the most quoted messianic psalm of the Passion. Representative examples are listed; several categories contain many more.

## Songs of Worship
The psalms that lift the voice toward God.

- Hymns of Praise | Exalting God for who He is and what He has made | 8, 19, 33, 100, 103, 145–150
- Thanksgiving Psalms | Gratitude for a specific deliverance already given | 30, 34, 40, 66, 107, 116, 138
- Enthronement Psalms | “The Lord reigneth” — celebrating God as King over all | 47, 93, 95–99
- Songs of Zion | The beauty and security of the holy city and temple | 46, 48, 76, 84, 87, 122
- Creation Psalms | The heavens and the earth declaring their Maker | 8, 19, 29, 104

## Songs of Sorrow and Trust
The psalms wrung out of distress — and the confidence that answers it.

- Laments (Individual) | One sufferer crying out of trouble, often turning to hope | 3, 6, 13, 22, 42–43, 88, 130
- Laments (Communal) | The whole people mourning defeat or desolation | 44, 74, 79, 80, 137
- Penitential Psalms | Confession of sin and plea for mercy | 6, 32, 38, 51, 102, 130, 143
- Psalms of Trust | Quiet confidence in God’s care amid danger | 11, 16, 23, 27, 62, 91, 121, 125
- Imprecatory Psalms | Calling down God’s judgment on the wicked | 35, 69, 83, 109, 137

## Songs of the King, the Pilgrim, and the Wise
Psalms tied to the throne, the road to Zion, and the path of life.

- Royal Psalms | The Lord’s anointed king — his reign, wars, and wedding | 2, 18, 20, 21, 45, 72, 110
- Messianic Psalms | Pointing beyond the king to the Christ (see companion chart) | 2, 16, 22, 40, 69, 110, 118
- Songs of Ascents | Pilgrim songs sung going up to Jerusalem | 120–134
- Wisdom Psalms | The two ways — the righteous and the wicked contrasted | 1, 37, 49, 73, 112, 127–128
- Torah Psalms | Delight in the law and word of the Lord | 1, 19, 119
- Historical Psalms | Retelling God’s dealings with Israel to teach the present | 78, 105, 106, 135, 136
