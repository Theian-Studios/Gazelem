# “And Thus We See”
* subtitle: Every editorial intrusion in the Book of Mormon and the lesson each one draws — compiled and verified from the text
* volume: book-of-mormon
* order: 2
* columns: Reference and voice | The verse | The lesson drawn
* widths: 1 | 2 | 1.6

The phrase “thus we see” (occasionally “thus we may see”) is the Book of Mormon’s signature editorial device — the moment a narrator or teacher steps out of the story to tell the reader what it means. It appears twenty-two times. Most belong to Mormon the abridger, but Nephi uses it first, Alma uses it as a teaching formula inside recorded sermons, and Moroni closes the pattern in his abridgment of Ether. Verses are excerpted to the relevant portion; ellipses mark trimmed text.

- **1 Nephi 16:29** // Nephi | 29 “…it was written and changed from time to time, according to the faith and diligence which we gave unto it. And **thus we see** that by small means the Lord can bring about great things.” | By small means the Lord brings about great things.
- **1 Nephi 17:3** // Nephi | 3 “And **thus we see** that the commandments of God must be fulfilled. And if it so be that the children of men keep the commandments of God he doth nourish them, and strengthen them, and provide means whereby they can accomplish the thing which he has commanded them…” | Those who keep the commandments are nourished, strengthened, and provided with means to obey.
- **Alma 12:22** // Alma, teaching Zeezrom | 22 “…Adam did fall by the partaking of the forbidden fruit, according to the word of God; and **thus we see**, that by his fall, all mankind became a lost and fallen people.” | By the fall, all mankind became a lost and fallen people.
- **Alma 24:19** // Mormon | 19 “And **thus we see** that, when these Lamanites were brought to believe and to know the truth, they were firm, and would suffer even unto death rather than commit sin; and **thus we see** that they buried their weapons of peace, or they buried the weapons of war, for peace.” | The truly converted are firm — willing to suffer death rather than sin.
- **Alma 24:27** // Mormon | 27 “…there were more than a thousand brought to the knowledge of the truth; **thus we see** that the Lord worketh in many ways to the salvation of his people.” | The Lord works in many ways — even through tragedy — for the salvation of his people.
- **Alma 28:13–14** // Mormon | 13 “And **thus we see** how great the inequality of man is because of sin and transgression, and the power of the devil…” // **14** “And **thus we see** the great call of diligence of men to labor in the vineyards of the Lord; and **thus we see** the great reason of sorrow, and also of rejoicing…” | Sin produces vast inequality among men — hence the urgent call to labor, and the reason for both sorrow and rejoicing.
- **Alma 30:60** // Mormon | 60 “And **thus we see** the end of him who perverteth the ways of the Lord; and **thus we see** that the devil will not support his children at the last day, but doth speedily drag them down to hell.” | The devil will not support his children at the last day.
- **Alma 42:4, 7, 14** // Alma, teaching Corianton | 4 “And **thus we see**, that there was a time granted unto man to repent, yea, a probationary time…” // **7** “…and **thus we see** they became subjects to follow after their own will.” // **14** “And **thus we see** that all mankind were fallen, and they were in the grasp of justice…” | Mortality is a probationary time; man is free to follow his own will; and all mankind, fallen, stand in the grasp of justice — the conditions that make the atonement necessary.
- **Alma 46:8–10** // Mormon — the war chapters | 8 “**Thus we see** how quick the children of men do forget the Lord their God, yea, how quick to do iniquity, and to be led away by the evil one.” // **9** “Yea, and **we also see** the great wickedness one very wicked man can cause to take place among the children of men.” | Men forget the Lord with astonishing speed — and one very wicked man can cause great wickedness.
- **Alma 50:19–23** // Mormon — the war chapters | 19 “And **thus we see** how merciful and just are all the dealings of the Lord, to the fulfilling of all his words unto the children of men…” // **23** “But behold there never was a happier time among the people of Nephi, since the days of Nephi, than in the days of Moroni…” | The covenant promise to Lehi is verified: the faithful are delivered and prosper — even in the middle of a war.
- **Helaman 3:27–28** // Mormon | 27 “**Thus we may see** that the Lord is merciful unto all who will, in the sincerity of their hearts, call upon his holy name.” // **28** “Yea, **thus we see** that the gate of heaven is open unto all, even to those who will believe on the name of Jesus Christ…” | The Lord is merciful to all who sincerely call on him; the gate of heaven is open to all who believe.
- **Helaman 6:34–36** // Mormon | 34 “And **thus we see** that the Nephites did begin to dwindle in unbelief… while the Lamanites began to grow exceedingly in the knowledge of their God…” // **35** “And **thus we see** that the Spirit of the Lord began to withdraw from the Nephites, because of the wickedness and the hardness of their hearts.” // **36** “And **thus we see** that the Lord began to pour out his Spirit upon the Lamanites, because of their easiness and willingness to believe in his words.” | The Spirit withdraws from hard hearts and pours out on those easy and willing to believe — regardless of lineage.
- **Helaman 6:40** // Mormon | 40 “And **thus we see** that they were in an awful state, and ripening for an everlasting destruction.” | A people who choose wickedness ripen toward destruction.
- **Helaman 12:3** // Mormon | 3 “And **thus we see** that except the Lord doth chasten his people with many afflictions… they will not remember him.” | Without chastening and affliction, the people will not remember the Lord.
- **Ether 14:25** // Moroni | 25 “And **thus we see** that the Lord did visit them in the fulness of his wrath, and their wickedness and abominations had prepared a way for their everlasting destruction.” | A civilization’s own wickedness prepares the way for its destruction.

## Patterns in the commentary

The cluster is thickest in Alma and Helaman — the era Mormon narrates most closely — and nearly absent from the war chapters themselves: across twenty chapters of battle, Mormon editorializes only twice (Alma 46:8–10 and 50:19–23), and both times about spiritual causes rather than tactics. Where he stays silent is as telling as where he speaks.

Read in sequence, the lessons darken. Nephi’s are about providence — small means and divine nourishment. Alma’s mid-book uses are doctrinal, laying out the fall, probation, and justice. From Helaman 6 onward, every single instance concerns decline: the Spirit withdrawing, the government captured, chastening by affliction, and finally — in Moroni’s hand — a civilization’s everlasting destruction. Mormon’s commentary is itself a plot arc, tracing the same trajectory as the people it describes, and Ether 14:25 stands as the Jaredite echo of where the Nephite story is headed.
